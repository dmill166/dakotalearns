## The project

**Dakota Learns** is what it says: a place where I learn in public and share it, free and beginner-first. *Let's learn something together.*

**Right now the focus is computer science** — full, free courses designed to be *portable* (recognizable as solid CS anywhere), aligned to recognized standards (ABET, ACM/IEEE-CS CS2023), and honest about what they do and don't cover. But the project isn't defined by one subject. It started as a personal blog in 2022 — covering everything from databases to security to the occasional life lesson — sparked by a simple idea from the data community: get involved, share what you learn, help others on the same journey. CS is where my energy is today (and where I'm headed as a teacher); if I'm learning something else worth sharing, it may show up here too.

**"Built in public" means exactly that.** I'm doing this on top of everything else life holds — a full-time job as a staff data engineer, being a husband, a dog dad, a grandson, a brother, a son. No fancy equipment, either: everything here was made with a laptop and an internet connection. I work in bursts (my ADHD and I have an arrangement), so material often gets built ahead and released on a cadence — but every piece goes out exactly the way I built it, with my review on everything. If I can do this, you can learn this.

## Who's behind it

Hi there — I'm **Dakota Hollmann**. By day I'm a staff data engineer who loves taking complex problems apart until they're in their simplest, most atomic pieces. Away from the keyboard, I'm spending time with my wife and our dog, watching a show, or plotting my next baking venture in the kitchen.

I have 10+ years in data and analytics, including architecting enterprise-scale telemetry pipelines (160B+ rows/hour) and contributing — from authoring and architecture to code review — across reporting that serves 30M+ customers. I hold an **MBA in Data Analytics** (Florida Gulf Coast University) and a **BS in Computer Science** with a math minor (Metropolitan State University of Denver). And I've been working since I was fourteen — GameStop, pizza shops, a college bookstore, overnight stock, tech support — before any of it said "engineer" on the badge. Those early jobs taught me things I still use every week, and I try never to forget where I started.

## Why teaching

Teaching was the plan before tech was. My very first degree is an Associate of Arts & Sciences in *education* — the original goal was high school math teacher, then eventually professor. Life rerouted me a few times (I changed majors eight times — yes, I counted), and I worked 40–60 hour weeks all the way through school. The destination never changed: my passion is helping people *understand* things.

I'm starting with computer science because computing isn't the future anymore — it's the present, and it will keep being the present. Whether you're eyeing a degree, teaching yourself toward a job, or just curious, I want you to be able to succeed, and I'd love to be one of the reasons you do. I also think there's never been a better time to learn: the information age plus honest use of AI as a study tool puts more within reach than ever.

I give back to the program I came from, too: I serve on **MSU Denver's Computer Science and Data Science & Machine Learning advisory boards**, have delivered guest lectures on databases and technical-interview prep, and am preparing to teach as adjunct faculty. This site is part of that preparation — the materials I'm building to teach from, shared openly so anyone can learn from them.
